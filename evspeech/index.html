Learning to Reconstruct Speech with an Event Camera

Yianni Karabatis, Levi Burner, Eadom Dessalene, Matthew Nolan, Christopher A. Metzler, Cornelia Fermuller, Ramani Duraiswami, Yiannis Aloimonos

University of Maryland, College Park

Abstract

This work introduces the first event-based visual microphone method trained on real-world data. Specifically, an event camera observes defocused laser speckle reflected from a chip bag placed near a loudspeaker, and a network maps the event stream to a log-mel spectrogram. Prior work relies on hand-derived models or simulation-based training, which cannot capture the idiosyncratic responses of real-world objects to sound. We overcome this challenge by training on microsecond-synchronized events and audio. Compared to prior work, our method's performance significantly exceeds SOTA methods qualitatively and quantitatively. This is because training accounts for an object's real-world frequency response, allowing the network to learn priors useful at inference. Our results demonstrate a clear advantage to using experimental data in the visual microphone problem.
